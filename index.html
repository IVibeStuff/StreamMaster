15-Stage Mastering Chain

▼

Prepares your WAV for streaming upload or personal listening. The chain runs automatically — EQ, air restoration, spectral dehaze, M/S widening, compression, transient shaping, loudness normalisation, and true-peak limiting.

Streaming outputs −14 LUFS / −1 dBTP — bright and forward, calibrated to survive Spotify, Apple Music, and Tidal's encoding. Local outputs −16 LUFS / −2 dBTP — warmer, for personal listening without platform processing.

Use this when:

  • Final step before uploading to a streaming platform
  • Producing a personal listening copy alongside the streaming master
  • After De-Jinx or Repair — always master the repaired file, not the raw Suno export

💡 Run De-Jinx and Repair first. Master last.

What it does

High shelf EQ at 10 kHz. Adds air and brightness above the vocal presence range.

When to change it

Leave at +1.5 dB for most tracks. Reduce towards 0 if the master sounds harsh. Increase to +3 dB for tracks that feel dull.

What it does

Narrow peak cut at 380 Hz. Removes the "boxy" or "muddy" quality common in Suno output.

When to change it

Leave at −2 dB for most tracks. Increase to −3 or −4 dB for dense low-mid congestion. Reduce towards 0 if the track sounds thin after mastering.

What it does

Restores a subtle high-frequency noise floor above 16 kHz, filling the hard spectral cutoff that Suno's generation creates.

When to change it

Leave at 0.018 for most tracks. Increase slightly (0.03) for tracks that feel very closed-in. Set to 0 to disable — some tracks sound better without it.

What it does

Breaks up the flat diffusion haze in the 8–16 kHz range that Suno introduces, using subtle amplitude modulation.

When to change it

Leave at ±4% for most tracks. Reduce to 0 if the track loses spaciousness after mastering — the haze can contribute positively to perceived width.

What it does

Input drive level for the parallel harmonic saturation stage. Higher drive adds more harmonic content.

When to change it

Leave at +6 dB for most tracks. Increase for tracks that feel sterile. Reduce if the master sounds congested.

What it does

Wet/dry blend of the saturation stage. 15% means 85% clean + 15% saturated signal.

When to change it

Leave at 15% for most tracks. Increase to 25–30% for acoustic or jazz tracks. Reduce to 5–10% for electronic tracks where cleanliness matters.

What it does

Threshold for the mid-channel glue compressor. Signals above this level are gently compressed.

When to change it

Leave at −18 dBRMS for most tracks. Lower to −22 for very dynamic tracks. Raise to −14 if the track feels pumping or over-compressed.

What it does

Compression ratio for the glue compressor. 2:1 is transparent and barely audible.

When to change it

Leave at 2:1 for most tracks. Increase to 3:1 for more dynamic control. Reduce to 1.5:1 for tracks with naturally good dynamics.

What it does

Controls how much sub-bass (below 120 Hz) stays in the stereo side channel. Lower values anchor the bass to the centre for a tighter, more mono-compatible low end.

When to change it

Leave at 15% for most tracks — this tightens bass for streaming. Increase towards 100% if the track has intentional wide sub-bass effects (distortion that sits "outside the ears", wide synth bass) that feel narrowed after mastering.

What it does

Restores the attack transients that Suno's internal compression flattens. Adds punch to drums and percussive elements.

When to change it

Leave at +2.5 dB for most tracks. Increase to +4–5 dB if drums feel soft or buried. Reduce to 0 for ambient tracks where transient punch is not desirable.

What it does

Dynamic EQ that cuts 2–4 kHz when it becomes too prominent. Reduces harshness in the upper-mid presence range.

When to change it

Leave at 3 dB for most tracks. Increase to 5–6 dB for aggressive or nasal-sounding vocals. Reduce to 0–1 dB if vocal presence is already well balanced.

What it does

Dynamic compressor above 5 kHz. When the overall high-frequency energy exceeds the threshold, the entire top end is gently reduced. Different from the de-esser — this targets broad high-frequency harshness rather than specific sibilant consonants.

When to change it

Leave Off for most tracks. Set to −28 dBRMS for tracks with a generally sharp, cutting, or harsh high-end character. The de-esser handles specific S/T consonants; this handles overall top-end brightness.

Section Replacement

▼

Replaces a specific time range with new audio — either a section regenerated in Suno's editor, or a different clip entirely. The tool handles level-matching and crossfading at both cut points so the join sounds natural.

This is the most surgical repair option: if De-Jinx and Repair can't fix a section, regenerate just that section in Suno and splice it in here.

Use this when:

  • A section is too corrupted for phase repair to fix — especially if the mid channel is also damaged
  • You've regenerated a section in Suno's editor and want to combine it with the original
  • The key changes unexpectedly at a Suno boundary and you want to replace the transition

💡 Cut points should be set in clean audio on both sides of the fault — at least 0.5s away from the problem.

Seam Smoothing

▼

Smooths the amplitude join at a point where Suno has already performed an internal section replacement. Suno's own Extend and Replace features sometimes leave a detectable level step or timbral shift at the boundary.

Heal applies a short crossfade centred on the seam timestamp. It works on level discontinuities — if the problem is phase corruption rather than a level step, use the Repair tab instead.

Use this when:

  • Suno's internal Replace or Extend left an audible click or level step
  • After using the Splice tab — to soften any remaining edge in the join
  • A mastered file has a subtle level shift at a known timestamp

💡 Heal works on amplitude. If the join sounds hollow or phase-y rather than clicky or steppy, it's a phase issue — use Repair, not Heal.

For tracks where the replacement is already baked in. Mark where the replaced section starts and ends — the tool smooths both seams.

Level Bridge

▼

Rides the gain across a quiet passage to bring it up to match the surrounding level. Useful when Suno generates an unexpectedly quiet verse or bridge, or when a splice creates a level imbalance between sections.

The tool analyses the level on both sides of the quiet zone and applies a smooth gain automation curve that interpolates between them.

Use this when:

  • A section is noticeably quieter than the rest of the track
  • A splice created a level step that Heal didn't fully resolve
  • Suno generated a verse at a different level to the chorus

💡 Set the start and end timestamps to the edges of the quiet zone, not the centre. The bridge fades in from the louder section on each side.

smooth gain ride S-curve interpolation no pumping

Use this after healing when the original track has a naturally quieter passage right after the splice. Select the quiet region — the tool rides the gain smoothly from the level before it to the level after it.

Synthesis Dropout Repair

▼

Suno's generation model occasionally loses coherence briefly — the audio collapses to near-silence for 80–300ms before recovering. These dropouts sound like a brief cut, mute, or glitch in an otherwise continuous section.

De-Jinx scans the full track, detects these collapses by measuring sudden level drops relative to the surrounding context, and repairs them using crossfaded content from adjacent clean audio. Every repair is shown with its exact timestamp and duration.

Use this when:

  • You hear a brief cut, click, or mute somewhere in the track
  • The Analyse tab reports synthesis dropouts detected
  • Before mastering — always De-Jinx first if dropouts are present

💡 Dropouts are different from boundary corruption (phase artefacts). If De-Jinx finds nothing but you still hear something wrong, try the Repair tab.

auto-detect micro-dropout repair full track scan

Automatically scans for Suno synthesis dropouts — brief moments where the audio collapses in level before snapping back. No timestamps needed; just drop the file and run.

Generation Boundary Repair

▼

Suno generates songs in sections. At the join between sections, the stereo field can become phase-incoherent — the left and right channels partially cancel each other, producing a hollow, "lifted", or flanging quality. This is distinct from a dropout and cannot be fixed by De-Jinx.

Phase repair (always available) isolates the corrupted zone, extracts the mid channel which is usually intact, reconstructs the stereo side channel from flanking clean content, and crossfades back. Stem repair (requires Demucs) separates the track into vocals and accompaniment before repairing each stem independently — better results on severely corrupted files.

Use this when:

  • You hear a hollow, "raised", or phase-y quality at a specific moment
  • Heal didn't fully fix a seam — the artefact may be phase corruption rather than a level join
  • The Repair tab shows multiple corruption zones — the song may have systematic boundary issues

💡 Suno's Remaster feature inherits boundary corruption from the original — remastering in Suno will not fix it. Use this tab instead.

phase repair boundary detection stem repair (Demucs)

Detects and repairs Suno generation boundary corruption — the phase inversion artefacts that cause a "lift" or hollow sound at section joins. Phase repair works immediately with no extra dependencies. Stem repair (requires Demucs) separates the track into vocals and accompaniment before repairing, giving better results on severely corrupted files.

Reconstructs the stereo side channel in corrupted zones using context from flanking clean sections. No additional dependencies required.

Stem Master

▼

Separates the track into four stems using Demucs (vocals, drums, bass, other), applies independent mastering settings to each, then recombines into a single polished output. This solves the core limitation of full-mix mastering: vocals and instrumentals often have different optimal processing needs.

Defaults are calibrated per stem — vocals get de-essing and gentle M/S widening, drums get stronger transient boost, bass preserves its full stereo width, and the melodic/other stem preserves instrumental character without over-processing.

💡 For tracks where the raw Suno instrumentals sound more impactful than the remaster, try setting M/S Width to 0 on the Other stem and Side Mix to 100% on the Bass stem — this preserves the original stereo character while still giving the vocals full processing.

Reference Track Matching

▼

Matches the tonal balance and loudness of your track to a commercially released reference using Matchering — an open-source mastering algorithm that analyses both files spectrally and adjusts EQ and level accordingly.

This is a complement to the Master tab, not a replacement. Master first to hit −14 LUFS and correct Suno's spectral issues, then use Ref Match if you want the final result to sit closer to a specific commercial sound.

Use this when:

  • You have a specific commercial track you want your song to sound like
  • The mastered output sounds tonally different to your target genre
  • A&R or client has provided a reference track

💡 The reference track should be a commercially released WAV or high-quality file in the same genre and tempo range as your track. Mismatched references produce unpredictable results.
